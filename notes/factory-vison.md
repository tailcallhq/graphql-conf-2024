## Narrative

Q1: What problem does ForgeCode solve?
A: ForgeCode helps organizations scale engineering output. Today, engineering output is capped by headcount: to ship more, you hire more engineers. ForgeCode removes that cap.

Q2: How does ForgeCode help me scale engineering output?
A: ForgeCode is an AI-native software factory. Your team sets the goals; ForgeCode continuously builds, tests, and deploys software to meet them. It measures its own work and reports progress against those goals, then uses the same metrics to improve itself, so each cycle is faster and more reliable than the last.

Q3: How is ForgeCode different from tools like Cursor and Claude Code?
A: These tools make each developer faster, but scaling your org still means hiring more developers. ForgeCode flips that model: AI takes on entire engineering roles, so you scale effortlessly, without increasing headcount.

Q4: With ForgeCode, will I not need developers anymore?
A: System accountability will always lie with humans. An engineering team will be required to set goals and adjust trade-offs for ForgeCode to deliver real outcomes.

Q5: Which roles will my org no longer need with ForgeCode?
A: Every role is affected, to a different degree. You no longer need dedicated QA, SDET, SRE, DevOps, or developer-productivity teams; ForgeCode takes over those roles entirely. Product engineering (frontend and backend) shrinks to a small core of engineers who direct ForgeCode. Decision-making roles, such as Principal/Staff engineers and engineering managers, aren't replaced; they move faster.

Q6: When ForgeCode breaks production, who is on call?
A: ForgeCode is on call 24/7/365. It joins the rotation like any new engineer: at first it needs guidance and learns from its mistakes. Soon it handles most incidents on its own and suggests design fixes across projects so they don't recur.

Q7: When ForgeCode ships a costly bug, who is responsible, and how do I know it won't happen again?
A: The same way you handle an engineer's mistake. Your team stays accountable, and ForgeCode runs the post-mortem: it reflects on what went wrong and proposes guardrails so it doesn't happen again. Because it has access to every past incident and failure, those proposals are grounded in data, not memory or opinion; the final call remains with the human in charge.

Q8: What does it take to hand a role over to ForgeCode?
A: You hand over a role by creating a Mission. A Mission has a goal and a way of working (its workflow). It can be private to one person or shared with your team. Work is tracked as issues that anyone on the Mission can add, update, or remove. Each Mission also gets its own working environment, a memory, and the tools it needs to deliver on its goal. As it works, it builds its memory and gets better at the role.
